Definitions

GM
Collected net revenue minus cost per test. No customer-paid fee is treated as a company cost.
Discount type
One of: campaign discount, member discount, coupon discount, wallet credit, gift card. A booking can carry several at once.
Stacked
The count of distinct discount types with a nonzero amount on one booking (0, 1, 2, or 3+).
Partial week
The most recent week, still accumulating bookings — excluded from every KPI, trend and week-over-week read.
Filter bar
Market, tier, channel and date-range selections apply to every chart and table that carries those dimensions; it does not retroactively change which weeks count as complete.

Assumptions editable



Other constants, defined in code and used consistently: simulator default volume-loss rate 15% (range 0–40%); coupon-risk bands Low<0.10 / Medium 0.10–0.25 / High>0.25; confidence tiers A/B/C per the rules in Pricing moves; break-even elasticity uses a 20% safety margin, direction-aware (see below).

Break-even elasticity logic

Break-even elasticity = ln((old price − cost)/(new price − cost)) ÷ ln(new price/old price) — the elasticity at which gross margin is unchanged by the move. A 20% safety margin is applied toward the conservative side: for a price increase, the move is judged to "clear" only if the estimated elasticity is less negative than break-even + 20% of its magnitude; for a decrease, only if it is more negative than break-even − 20% of its magnitude. This is a locally-linearised estimate, not a full demand-curve fit.

How this synthetic dataset was built

  • Stacking gradient — GM% steps down roughly 9 / 4 / 4 points as discount-type count rises from 0 to 1 to 2 to 3+, each bucket with n in the thousands.
  • Over-stack tail — one coupon (see Discount leakage → Over-stack tail) launches mid-period with a large share of its own bookings GM-negative: small in total dollars, concentrated in one place — the defensible "cap this" story.
  • A policy change — a discount type is switched off partway through the period (see the promotions timeline).
  • Eight executed price events, two deliberately confounded by a market-level coupon push that lands in the same window — the before/after tracker is built to flag exactly those two, not just report an average.

State plainly: every finding on this page is a property of this simulated dataset, demonstrating the method — not a result achieved for any client or employer.

Limitations

  • All tables are weekly aggregates; no booking-level drill-down is published here.
  • Elasticity estimates are noisy at low weekly volume — the 90% interval widens accordingly, and tiering is rule-based, not a formal power calculation.
  • Control-market validity assumes no unobserved market-specific shock besides what's in the promotions log — the confound check only catches what that log records plus a coupon-share heuristic.
  • No payer, insurance or claims data is modelled.
